# RAW noise reduction

`denoiseMosaic(gpu, mosaic, signal)` reduces a RAW photo's noise on its Bayer mosaic, before the GPU demosaics it, and resolves to a half-size `rgba16float` target holding each 2 × 2 cell's four samples by position, in sensor units. It runs without React, model weights, or workers, entirely in WGSL but for two small fits in TypeScript. The RAW source keeps the result until it closes; a pass blends the decoded samples toward it by the image layer's amount and demosaics the blend, so fading or editing the layers above never reduces noise again.

1. **Noise model.** Every 32 × 32 block of sensor pixels gives each cell position its mean and the variance of its diagonal Haar detail, which flat blocks owe to noise alone. Over 40 equal-count bins along the signal, the quietest quarter of each bin gives a point, and a weighted line through them is the position's Poisson-Gaussian model, variance = gain · signal + floor. Positions fit apart: some cameras scale a channel digitally, which scales its noise too. A block with a clipped sample is skipped.
2. **Spectrum.** The generalized Anscombe transform, measured from its value at black so half floats stay precise, makes each position's noise unit Gaussian, signal-dependent noise included. An orthonormal transform then turns red, the two greens, and blue into light, green against magenta, red against blue, and the greens' difference, keeping that noise unit. A sample far above its same-color neighbors and every adjacent one is a hot pixel, since light reaches neighbors through the lens; it takes its brightest same-color neighbor's value. Samples below the transform's lowest value, such as dead pixels, are clamped there.
3. **Pyramid.** Averaging 2 × 2 texels builds up to six levels, down to 32 texels. Each level measures its own noise per component from its flattest blocks rather than assuming it halves: lossy RAW compression, for one, leaves noise that is quieter at fine scales and louder at coarse ones, which an assumed halving turns into blotches.
4. **Filter.** From the coarsest level up, sliding 8 × 8 DCT denoising runs in two steps: hard thresholding at 3 deviations, 3.5 for the greens' difference, then Wiener shrinkage by the first estimate, with color shrinking twice and the greens' difference eight times as hard as light. Every patch position counts, each output texel gathers the 64 patches over it in workgroup memory, weighted by how little of each survived and by a Kaiser window. A patch's DC stays whole, so every level stays unbiased, except the greens' difference: both greens see the same light, so its mean is noise or imbalance, which demosaicing would draw as a maze. After each step, the upsampled difference between the coarser estimate and the step's own 2 × 2 averages, blurred, replaces the step's low frequencies: broad noise the 8 × 8 patches cannot tell from the image goes, the coarser level's finest frequencies, where its patches ring at edges, stay out, and where the two levels agree nothing changes.
5. **Inverse.** The orthonormal transform back, and the closed-form inverse that is unbiased for Poisson-Gaussian noise (Mäkitalo and Foi), return sensor samples, so shadows keep their level after a strong exposure push.

Patches are filtered alone. Grouping similar ones, as BM3D does, ranks candidates by a distance that noise dominates in dark areas; along edges it groups aligned patches whose average keeps a profile of noise, which showed as streaks. Alone, a patch cannot mistake aligned noise for likeness.

## Limits

Detail with less signal than noise is gone for any filter: in the darkest areas of a high-ISO photo the result is smooth rather than textured. Very fine repeated texture near the noise level softens. The model assumes Poisson-Gaussian noise; row or column banding, which some sensors add, passes through. Only 2 × 2 color filter mosaics that the GPU demosaics take part; X-Trans and linear DNG files have no noise reduction.

## Verification

`tests/noise-reduction.test.ts` checks the noise fit against blocks of known noise, a third of them textured. `tests/noise-reduction.e2e.ts` opens a RAW fixture with seeded Poisson-Gaussian noise, reduces it, and checks that its deviation falls well below the noise while its mean holds, and that fading back to 0 develops the decoded samples again.
